What control means for an IPO buyer

  • No change of control without the founders: the Founder LLC votes as one block, decided one vote per continuing founder; public holders can never reach a majority.
  • The board answers to the Trust: Class T directors are a majority of authorized seats, and the Trust's terms change only with supermajorities or founder consent.
  • Strategics own without voting: Amazon and Google hold non-voting Class N stock, convertible to Class A only on sale to an unaffiliated party.
  • Dilution ratchet: up to a further 2% to the Public Benefit Fund each time the share price rises 50% above the prior milestone.
  • Lock-up: co-founders and holders above 0.5% are locked up until after the Q3 2027 earnings release; five co-founders are selling stockholders in the IPO.

08

Strategy

An enterprise-first research lab whose products (Claude Platform, Claude Code, Cowork) are channels for model capability. Governance and control are covered in sections 06 and 07.

Growth strategy (S-1)

Seven stated pillars

  • Talent & culture: mission-aligned, low-ego research culture.
  • Compute: capacity beyond current deals, own data centers ("Anthropic Direct"), multi-chip fleet.
  • Capability & safety together: "closing the loop", each model helps build the next.
  • Accessibility: every surface, all three hyperscaler clouds.
  • Adoption: direct sales, forward-deployed Applied AI, SIs, Ode with Anthropic.
  • New markets: life sciences, robotics, physical work.
  • M&A and partnerships, selectively.

Anthropic's moat, as a banker would pitch it

  • Enterprise and coding leadership rather than consumer reach: revenue is mostly consumption from businesses, not chat subscriptions.
  • Multi-chip compute (Trainium, TPU, NVIDIA, AMD) reduces single-vendor pricing power, though the vendors are also rivals.
  • Trust and safety brand as a buying criterion in regulated industries.
  • Weak spots: no owned cloud, no consumer distribution at Google or OpenAI scale, and open-weight models compress prices at the low end.

Bull case

Fastest enterprise ramp on record

  • Revenue grows far faster than compute; coverage of compute cost rose roughly fivefold in five quarters.
  • GAAP operating profit and positive operating cash flow before IPO.
  • Extreme net expansion and compounding large-account counts.
  • Cash pile funds years of operations before IPO proceeds.
Bear case

A decade of fixed costs on a quarter of evidence

  • Commitments are many times annual revenue and largely non-cancelable.
  • Q3 growth slowed sharply; most revenue comes from models less than six months old.
  • Margins benefit from compute bought 12–18 months ahead; new capacity arrives before revenue.
  • Public holders get little say; SBC spikes at IPO.
Before pricing

Diligence checklist

  • Q3 GAAP figures and the Oct 31 run-rate.
  • Training vs inference split; a pro forma gross margin.
  • Revenue net of marketplace fees; consideration payable to CSP customers.
  • Commitments due in the next 12 months vs cash generation.
  • Cohort NRR excluding the top-5 customers.
